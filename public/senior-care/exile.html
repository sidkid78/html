Why the Greatest Threat to Aging is the House Itself

The Tragedy of the "Last Trip"

Every year, millions of older Americans take a "last trip" out of their front door. It isn't a vacation or a planned move. It’s an ambulance ride. The cause isn't a virus or a disease—it’s a rug that slipped, a stair that was too steep, or a bathroom that turned into a trap.

In that moment, a lifetime of independence vanishes. The home, once a sanctuary of memories, becomes the catalyst for exile to an institutional bed. This isn't just a "demographic shift." It is a silent, nationwide eviction of our elders.

The Blind Spot in Our Care

We spend billions on heart monitors and medications, yet we ignore the "Built Environment"—the very stage upon which the drama of aging is played.

The current system for fixing this is broken. We rely on manual, subjective "walk-throughs" that are too slow, too inconsistent, and too hard for families to visualize. By the time a traditional home audit is filed, the fall has often already happened. We are fighting a 21st-century crisis with 19th-century clipboards.

Executive Summary

Starting a home health agency in the current economic climate requires a blend of compassionate medical support and cutting-edge operational efficiency. This plan outlines the launch of HOMEase, a proposed C-Corp startup designed to bridge the gap between traditional skilled nursing and modern, tech-integrated safety evaluations.


By focusing on skilled rehabilitation therapy and palliative support, HOMEase aims to address the critical shortage of high-quality in-home services in Central Texas, utilizing a lean startup model to navigate the rigorous regulatory environment and achieve a sustainable, five-year growth trajectory.

Market Context:
The Central Texas Demand

The rolling hills of Central Texas, spanning from the bustling corridors of Temple and Belton to the expanding residential areas of Killeen, are witnessing a demographic shift. As the regional population ages, a powerful preference has emerged: the desire to "age in place." This trend is more than a lifestyle choice; it is a clinical necessity, as home-based care often leads to better recovery outcomes and reduced hospital readmissions.

The HOMEase Competitive Edge

HOMEase is more than a service provider; it is a proprietary, tech-enhanced care model. The agency differentiates itself through integrated home assessment technology used during the intake process.

Clinicians utilize this platform to generate comprehensive safety reports, identifying fall risks and recommending specific modifications. This data-driven approach builds immediate trust with referral sources.

By positioning the agency as a strategic partner in the healthcare continuum, HOMEase moves beyond commodity services to become a high-value clinical asset.

Navigating the Texas Regulatory Landscape: HCSSA Licensing

Operating a home health agency in Texas requires strict adherence to the Health and Community Support Services Agency (HCSSA) standards managed by the Texas Health & Human Services Commission (HHSC). The first step is selecting the appropriate service categories via the Texas Unified Licensure Information Portal (TULIP).

  • Designation: Licensed Home Health Services (LHHS) for skilled medical care.
  • Leadership: Appointment of an Administrator and Alternate Administrator.
  • Training: 8-hour initial course + 12 hours annual CE.
  • Fee: $2,625 initial licensing fee.

The Path to Medicare and Medicaid Certification

While a state license allows for private-pay operations, Medicare and Medicaid certification is essential for long-term scalability. To bill Medicare, the agency must undergo a survey to ensure compliance with federal Conditions of Participation.

...Because the state-led survey process can involve significant backlogs, many Central Texas startups pursue accreditation through private bodies like CHAP or ACHC.

Lean Staffing:
The W-2 and Contractor Hybrid Model

To maintain a lean operation during the first 24 months, a hybrid staffing model is recommended.

Core Team (2-5 FTEs)

High-performing Administrator/Director of Nursing (DON) and at least one full-time RN Case Manager.

Variable Volume

Utilize PRN (as-needed) contractors for specialized roles such as Physical Therapists (PT) and Occupational Therapists (OT).

To ensure operational readiness, the agency will implement a systematic transition: once a specific discipline (e.g., Physical Therapy) reaches a consistent volume of 15-20 billable hours per week, the lead contractor will be offered a permanent W-2 role. This metric-based approach allows the agency to scale its fixed costs in direct proportion to its revenue growth.

Strategic Advantages for Native American Entrepreneurs

As a Native American-owned business, HOMEase can leverage unique federal and state resources to offset startup costs and gain a competitive foothold.

  • ✦SBA 8(a) Business Development Program: Advantage for securing government contracts (VA home care).
  • ✦Office of Native American Affairs (ONAA): Specialized training.
  • ✦Indian Health Service (IHS): Funding for community health initiatives.
  • ✦SBIR Program: Explore if home assessment technology involves novel R&D.

Go-To-Market Strategy:
Building a Referral-Based Ecosystem

Institutional Gatekeepers

Success depends on a robust referral network. The strategy focuses on hospital discharge planners and case managers at facilities like Baylor Scott & White. The marketing message emphasizes the HOMEase tech-driven safety assessment, which directly addresses the physician's fear of patient falls.

Private-Pay & The "Sandwich Generation"

Targeted digital advertising should focus on adult children aged 45-65 searching for reliable, high-tech solutions for their parents. By combining professional medical networking with community-based education, the agency can build a pipeline that sustains growth through all five years.

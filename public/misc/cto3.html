In Hardware, Mistakes Are Permanent. In Senior Care, They’re Fatal.

In the world of hardware engineering, there is no "Control+Z." Once a trace is etched into a circuit board, it’s destiny. You learn very quickly to respect the "No-Undo" discipline.

I spent my life at soldering stations where a millimeter’s error meant total system failure.

Fragile Systems

I brought that same "hardware-grade" obsession to Homease | AI because we are dealing with a system far more fragile than a motherboard: the safety of a human being aging at home.

Most AI companies operate on the "move fast and break things" model. But when you are analyzing the home of a 80-year-old widow, "breaking things" isn't an option. A hallucination in our code isn't a UI glitch—it's a missed fall risk, a failed grab bar, or a forgotten medication trigger.

A Moral Obligation

My step-grandfather, Dr. L. James Lewis, worked on the team that solved the polio crisis. He didn't view chemistry as a series of experiments; he viewed it as a moral obligation to eliminate human suffering through systematic rigor.

At Homease, we aren't "playing" with agentic loops or testing a tech stack. We are building a safety net for the people who raised us. We apply the precision of hardware engineering and the legacy of clinical science to ensure that "aging in place" isn't a gamble—it's a guarantee.

Because for our parents and grandparents, there is no "undo" button. We have to get it right the first time.
